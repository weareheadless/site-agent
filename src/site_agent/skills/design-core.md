# Design Quality Core

This is the quality floor for every visual change. Existing brand conventions
are constraints, not permission to preserve weak hierarchy or inaccessible
styling.

## Intentional design

- Every design decision should carry the subject: the audience, the content,
  and the site's own world. A design that could belong to any business is not
  finished.
- Commit to one clear visual idea and carry it through every element. The idea
  comes from the brief: the confirmed vibe, palette, typography, imagery, and
  the business's own world. Never import a theme from a different subject.
- The quality bar is "high-end and one of a kind", not "clean and neutral".
  A competent-but-interchangeable page fails the bar even when it has no bugs.
- Spend boldness in one place: one memorable element, quiet discipline around
  it. Cut decoration that does not serve the brief.

## How to choose the one idea

1. Read the confirmed brief: audience, impression, vibe, voice, palette,
   typography, the images the owner actually supplied, and the primary action.
2. Find the single most characteristic thing in that subject's world and make
   it the spine of the page — the opening moment, the material, the rhythm, the
   signature interaction.
3. Everything else serves that spine. If an element could be swapped to another
   business without anyone noticing, it is not carrying the idea — redo or cut it.

## Hierarchy and composition

- One dominant hierarchy move per viewport. If two elements compete for first
  look, decide which wins and make it measurably dominant (scale, weight,
  contrast, or placement).
- Contrast is the primary hierarchy tool: size, weight, color, spacing.
  Whitespace is a feature, not a leftover.
- Balance asymmetry with structure: offset grids, pulled imagery, and
  full-bleed moments break sameness; do not center everything by default.
- Every section earns its place. If a section has no job, remove it.

## Depth and material

- Depth comes from layering surfaces and from light, never from default
  shadows. Tonal variation reads expensive; 4px gray borders read template.
- Meaningful transparency: glass, gradients, and overlays should reveal
  something, not hide a weak background.
- Texture is subtle: grain, fine lines, and material cues at low opacity that
  give the surface a physical feel.

## Typography

- Type carries the personality. Choose one characterful display face and one
  restrained body face, then set a real scale with intentional weights,
  widths, and optical spacing.
- Extreme scale contrast is drama: massive display against small, letter-spaced
  captions reads authored rather than default.
- Measure, line-height, and rhythm must be correct: body 45–75ch, line-height
  ≥1.5, headings ≤1.1 and tight.

## Copy

- Words are design material. Use the brief's real content; never lorem ipsum.
- Write from the visitor's side: plain verbs, sentence case, specific, no hype.
  A headline that could sit on any site is a missed opportunity to be the
  business.
- Buttons name the action ("Book the retreat", "Get a quote"), and the action
  keeps the same name through the whole flow.

## Quality checks

- Body text contrast is at least 4.5:1 and large text/icons at least 3:1;
  verify dark and translucent variants.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus, 44px hit areas, responsive behavior, and
  prefers-reduced-motion support.
- Content must never depend on JavaScript: core copy, navigation, and primary
  actions stay visible when scripts fail or motion is reduced.
- Before finishing, critique whether the result has depth, clear hierarchy,
  meaningful transparency, and a distinct visual signature on desktop and
  mobile.
- Ask: could this page belong to a different line of business with only a
  copy change? If yes to a significant degree, the design is not finished.
